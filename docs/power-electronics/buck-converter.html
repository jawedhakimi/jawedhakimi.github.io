Buck converter

Power electronics

A buck converter steps a DC input down to a lower DC output by switching an inductor between the input and ground, then filtering the result. This is the ideal, continuous-conduction-mode (CCM) analysis — enough to pick a starting inductor and output capacitor.

Duty cycle

Ignoring losses, the output is set entirely by duty cycle:

\[ D = \frac{V_{out}}{V_{in}} \]

Inductor selection

While the switch is on, the inductor sees \((V_{in} - V_{out})\) across it for time \(D/f_{sw}\); while off, it sees \(-V_{out}\) for \((1-D)/f_{sw}\). Either way gives the same ripple current:

\[ \Delta I_L = \frac{V_{out}(1 - D)}{f_{sw} L} \]

Solving for \(L\) at a target ripple (commonly 20–40% of \(I_{out}\)):

\[ L = \frac{V_{out}(1-D)}{f_{sw}\, \Delta I_L} \]

Output capacitor

Assuming the ripple is dominated by capacitance (not ESR), the output voltage ripple is approximately:

\[ \Delta V_{out} \approx \frac{\Delta I_L}{8\, f_{sw}\, C_{out}} \quad \Rightarrow \quad C_{out} = \frac{\Delta I_L}{8\, f_{sw}\, \Delta V_{out}} \]

Real capacitors also add ESR-driven ripple (\(\Delta V_{ESR} = \Delta I_L \cdot R_{ESR}\)) on top of this — worth checking separately against the datasheet ESR once a part is chosen.
